Where the two disagree about **what exists today**, this document wins and `VISION.md` is the bug; where they disagree about **where the project is going**, `VISION.md` wins and this document is the bug. ### Trust Layer, identity and Protocol APW

- `[x]` Web Bot Auth (RFC 9421, Ed25519) with shared draft-03 rules, key-directory cache, nonce uniqueness (shared across instances via D1/SQLite since v0.0.9.31 — in-memory fallback for single-process dev).

- `[x]` **Protocol APW v1.3 + SVRI v1.0** ([spec](./docs/protocol-apw/APW-SPEC-v1.3.md), [SVRI E-16](./docs/protocol-apw/SVRI-v1.0.md), [APW vectors](./docs/protocol-apw/APW-VECTORS-v1.3.md), [SVRI vectors](./docs/protocol-apw/vectors/svri/v1.0/)): DNS-over-HTTPS `did:apw`, signed artifacts and attestations, chained history, LTP dimension scoring, five evidence tiers, qualitative public SVRI, and protected numerical details/HSVR. - `[x]` **External witnesses (ERRATA E-10, spec §5.7)**: a site with identity observes another's chain and publishes a signed statement (`apw-witness+jws`, deterministic `jti`) at `/.well-known/apw-witnesses.json`, logged in its own chained history. Fail-closed evaluation: `head_mismatch`, `equivocation`, `backdated_entry`. Recorded via `POST /admin/api/witness/record`.
- `[x]` **Semi-automatic `h` anchoring**: TXT v2 computation with live `k`+`h` via `dnslink/cli.mjs --anchor --domain` and the dashboard checklist; witnesses anchor heads without touching DNS. Pasting the TXT into the DNS provider stays manual (no standardized DNS API).
- `[x]` **Automatic `reader_conduct` emission**: Trust Layer middleware emits on verified access (deterministic `jti`, `reader_conduct_jti` index with history seeding, per-isolate throttle, `APW_AUTO_EMIT_CONDUCT=false` opt-out). Ledger now records `allow` accesses too.
- `[x]` **Per-reader bypass (spec §6.5, closes E-8)**: Permissions Center grants `trust:read-governance-bypass` over a reader DID; `GET /trust/:siteId` passes them without thresholds. Does not skip APW identity (§6.3) or a corrupt policy; no store → no bypass.
- `[x]` **Non-active reader keys (spec §6.3)**: a signature whose fingerprint is not `k` is accepted if the key is in the reader's verified history, signed ≥1 chain entry, and its `[validFrom, validTo + 10 min grace]` window covers the request; otherwise `apw_unresolvable: key_not_bound_to_apw` and the cache TTL clamps to window close.
- `[x]` **Public W-dimension projection (spec §6.6, closes E-8)**: `GET /trust/:siteId` computes site dimensions from its snapshot and always publishes `projection { dimensions, aggregates, governed }`; on denial, only the public set (W-01, W-04, W-06, W-07, W-09) plus all aggregates.

### Derived-score rendering integrity (completed; see Trust Layer)

`VISION.md` states the rule — *a default must never quietly stand in for missing evidence*. The score renderers preserve it: `scoreSiteDimensions()` emits no entry for missing W evidence, the `/trust/atproto/:handle` projection omits zero-count/zero-weight R-05 priors and returns `no_data` when no measured reader dimensions exist, and the public Trust page reports counts rather than averaging visitor opinions into a reputation score. `scoreSubject()` and `scoreOf()` retain protocol defaults for internal calculations and policy evaluation; callers must carry their count and weight metadata rather than render those priors as observations.

- `[x]` **Audit derived-score renderers; do not present a prior or unscored opinion as reputation.** The public AT Protocol bridge now omits unmeasured zero-count/zero-weight dimensions and reports `no_data`; the public Trust page separates signed-atestation and unsigned-opinion counts instead of presenting an average of unsigned votes as a score. Empty `bayesianScore()` results and `scoreOf()` defaults remain available for protocol math/policy, with count/weight metadata; site projections already include only evidence-backed dimensions. Regression coverage: `atproto-reputation.test.ts`, `trust-page-honesty.test.ts`, `site-trust-public-verification.test.ts`, and `trust-w-projection.test.ts`.

### Protocol APW (remaining spec v1.3 items)

- `[ ]` **Enable R-05 in policies** — blocked by *data*, not config: R-05 is the calibration of attestations the reader *emits* (A.4.4), and `loadReaderConduct` only loads what it *received*; every reader today is R-05 = 4.0 with weight 0, so enabling `policyDimensions["R-05"]` would 403 everyone. Needs (1) a flow where readers emit attestations over subjects (today only anonymous community voting by IP, no DID) and (2) a corpus of other emitters on the same (subject, dimension) for leave-one-out consensus — indexes or witnesses (spec §7). Anchored in `tests/unit/apw-reader-conduct-config.test.ts`; documented in spec §6.5 and ERRATA E-9.
- `[ ]` **Signals `rate_respected`, `paid_as_agreed`, `redistribution`** (spec §6.4) — blocked by data: the ledger only records `requests_total` and `policy_violations_detected`; the middleware passes `charged: false` on all four paths. Each waits for its own input: a rate in the content policy plus rejection counters; a settlement that tells the origin whether the crawler actually paid (current Cloudflare PPC settles out-of-band); and witnesses or denunciations for redistribution, which the origin cannot observe.

Honest limits in `docs/architecture/identity-atproto.md`: resolution depends on third parties (DNS/plc.directory), discovery is suffix-only (the inverse "which site claims this foreign handle" index is Portaless Index, future), R is host-scoped, remote trust is best-effort; the whitepaper's own vision (own PDS per user, `com.portaless.comment` Lexicons, App View over the firehose) remains unimplemented by design.
- `[x]` Portaless Cloud Images — the cryptographic base is real and usable (`packages/cloud-images/`, v0.0.9.10): **Cloud Image v1 payload** (`contentHash` = sha256 over the sidecar-free bytes, `signedAt` inside the signed data, contemporaneous public SiteTrustScore, `declaredOrigin` human|ai|mixed) + sidecar `{payload, jws}` signed server-side with the site's active Ed25519 key (`typ: apw-image+jws`, JCS via `signSiteJws`; payload tamper-evident by canonical-byte equality; kid must belong to the payload's siteDid, legacy RFC 7638 thumbprint kids accepted) + embedding in a PNG private `pAPW` chunk (insert/replace before IEND, CRC-checked extract, byte-exact strip — the image travels with its own signed metadata) + `cloud_images` store (D1/SQLite/InMemory, unique `content_hash` = dedupe) + `POST /admin/api/cloud-images` (admin role server-side, automatic signing with `getActiveSigningKey`, 404 `site_identity_not_found` without identity, PNG-only, 4 MiB cap) + public serve `GET /images/:imageId` (ETag=contentHash, immutable cache, 304) + local verification `GET /trust/images/:imageId` (against `site_identity` `listKeys`, so it survives key rotation) + standalone verification `POST /trust/images/verify` that resolves the site's keys against the chained APW registry (history including retired keys, did.json fallback) — C2PA without a central authority, never throwing (structured `reason`s, `content_hash_mismatch` when the bytes don't match). ~56 new tests across 7 suites; `schema-additions/cloud-images.sql` joined to master `schema.sql` and `REQUIRED_TABLES`. Honest limits in `docs/architecture/cloud-images.md`: PNG-only transport in v1 (jpeg/webp/gif are in the format but have no sidecar channel), 4 MiB in DB (R2 is the external item), `declaredOrigin` is a declaration, not a detection (moderation of false declarations is future work), no UI, EXIF neither read nor stripped.

## Vision and scope decisions (what Portaless will NOT do)

- **Plugin ecosystem**: open catalog, community-regulated. Portaless does not certify plugins; it exposes a public, auditable community `trustScore` and `auditedBy` as informational data. The sandbox limits what a plugin *can* do; it does not certify what it *does*.
- **Trust Layer / Pay per Crawl**: an open protocol, not an insurer. Portaless defines and exposes the protocol (cryptographic agent identification, `allow`/`charge`/`block` policies, public ledger); it never collects money — third parties build settlement layers on top (AppPlace candidates, never core).
- **Site-generation agent**: via an external SDK project, never in the AGPL core (no license mixing, no coupled release cycles).
- **Portaless Index — site discovery**: the local witness aggregator already exists (see production section); the cross-site discovery layer (indexability format reusing the APW TXT, third-party query permission — public / attributed / via settlement, manifest aggregation, provenance layer before treating community votes as sufficient) remains future work. Index is also the first natural reader of the enclaves.
- **One-click onboarding**: third-party-dependent steps (DNS propagation, foreign infrastructure) live in the deferred dashboard checklist, never in a blocking wizard. Remaining internal gaps: visual draft preview of step 1, generic text outside the intent field, Cloudflare Pages OAuth (GitHub only today).
- **Cloud Images + APW as cryptographic SSOT**: the cryptographic base landed in v0.0.9.10 (see the `[x]` item above): image hosting where each image carries the site's Ed25519 signature, a cryptographic existence timestamp, the contemporaneous SiteTrustScore, and a declared-origin flag (`human` | `ai` | `mixed`) — C2PA without a central authority, verifiable against the APW registry, both locally and standalone. The `key_algorithm` column prepares the ML-DSA migration without breaking the chain of custody. Still future: external storage (R2) for large images, moderation model for mis-declared images, EXIF handling, sidecar transport for non-PNG formats, and a management UI.
- **Videos module**: the `Video` element exists as an embed, but a channel, a TikTok source and any ranking do not. A ranking needs a **view registry**, which this repository does not have. Not promised until that registry exists.
- **Music module**: nothing exists but static links. **Before anything is communicated**: there is no public route to *stream plays* from Spotify or Apple Music. Any "consolidated global impact" must be defined from what is actually obtainable — followers, chart positions, top tracks, releases — or it is a claim a customer can disprove in one call.
- **Assisted 5-minute installation**: a **provider service, not a core promise**. The steps that depend on third parties (a GitHub account, a Cloudflare account, DNS propagation) live in the assistant and the documentation, never in a blocking wizard the core cannot complete alone. Someone who wants it free does it in ~30 minutes following tutorials; someone who wants it in 5 minutes buys it from a provider (Cloudum or another). What the core does guarantee: an honest guide to what is needed — GitHub, Cloudflare, and an MCP client connected to the AI — an assistant that explains each step and why it exists, and a local install (`npm run setup`, Docker) that depends on nobody.

### Classified backlog: directions that are not commitments today

The taxonomy (`NOW` / `ENABLE` / `GATED` / `OUT OF CORE`) is defined in [`docs/VISION.md`](./docs/VISION.md). Applied to the directions that keep coming back in proposals: none of these rows is a rejection — deferral is a sequencing decision, and it is reversible the moment its condition is met — and every `GATED` row names the evidence that would move it. **The condition is the commitment; the item is not.**

| Direction | Class | Condition to move |
|---|---|---|
| Portable community reputation — a Veracier's history readable from its own chain, not only from the evaluated site's table | `GATED` | Phase 8's recorded scope limit: E-13 deliberately chose the evaluated site's table over the emitter's chain. Moving needs (1) the reader fetching and verifying the emitter's own APW chain, and (2) more than one real emitter for it to matter. |
| Cross-node reconciliation | `GATED` | An **observed** conflict class between independently operated nodes — stale state, duplicate subject, ownership ambiguity — reproduced in a test first. No engine before the conflict exists. |

| Universal canonical-state schema | `OUT OF CORE` | Deferred by design, not designed up front. | Public `/explore.json.js` (an unauthenticated endpoint that fans out to N peers) | `GATED` | Peers that actually publish `/.well-known/apw-pages.jws`. ## Roadmap to the official Beta — phases 0 to 6

> **Everything in this section must be true before the official Beta**, and its exit criterion is one fact: a person outside the project, without the author's help, installs Portaless, publishes a site with APW identity, and that site appears in an Index node that is not the author's (Phase 5).
>
> Phase numbering is a **dependency order, not a sequence of deliveries**: phases were split further as work revealed more (0.5, 7–11), and that is the normal case. No dates: this is a single-maintainer project, and a date would be a false promise. Each phase carries a **verifiable exit criterion** — advancing to the next depends on a fact, not a feeling, the same standard this ROADMAP already uses with its PRs.

### Phase 0 — Visual identity foundation (precondition for everything else)

**Goal**: one source of truth for color, applied to dashboard and public site alike, with the official palette as the default for new installs.

**Why first**: no later UI phase makes sense if it is built on two color systems that do not talk to each other. Doing it now avoids rework.

1. Extend `DesignTokens` (`skin-engine/tokens.ts`) so `src/styles/global.css` reads the same CSS variables (`--bg`, `--text`, `--accent`, …) instead of its current hardcoded values: the public site becomes governed by the same `skin.json` that today only governs the dashboard. **Honest cost note**: the 12 editor elements render with hardcoded inline `style="..."` (`registry.ts`), not variables — making the public site respond to the skin requires refactoring that `renderHTML` to variables, not just wiring `global.css`.
2. Set `base.json` with the official palette as the default for new installs, with the contrast fix already resolved (text never on pure `#C5D86D` over a light background).
3. Add a real dark mode using `#261C15` as `--bg` and `#F7F7F2` as `--text`.
4. A theme selector in `/admin` that writes the active skin, so "edit later" is a form field, not hand-editing JSON.

**Exit criterion**: change `accent` in one place (`skin.json`) and see it reflected in dashboard, editor, and public site without touching CSS by hand, confirmed by a simple before/after visual regression test.

**Status: shipped.** `global.css` now reads the token variables (`--bg`, `--text`, `--accent`, …) with the official palette as default and aliases the historical `--portaless-*` names to them; the `atomic-elements` `renderHTML` was refactored from hardcoded hex to `var(--token, fallback)` (the honest cost note above); `DEFAULT_TOKENS`/`base.json` carry the official light palette plus a new `accentText` token (the readable-as-text variant of the lime accent — contrast rule enforced); a real dark mode (`#261C15`/`#F7F7F2`) ships via `prefers-color-scheme` + `data-theme`; the active skin persists in a new `site_skin` table served to every page (public site, dashboard, editor) as a stylesheet from `GET /api/site-skin`; and `/admin/appearance` writes it (skin picker + color overrides + per-viewer light/dark toggle). Verified by unit tests plus a Playwright before/after test (`tests/browser/appearance-theme.spec.ts`).

### Phase 0.5 — Close the edit→publish cycle

**Goal**: saving a page in the editor makes it visible to visitors without a terminal.

**Why it exists**: today `PUT /admin/pages/:slug` writes to D1/SQLite, but `/pages/[slug]` is built only from `src/content/pages/*.json` at build time — nothing serves the DB at runtime, so an edited page is invisible to every visitor until someone runs `npm run publish` (a CLI that only supports self-hosted SQLite and has never been run against a real GitHub account). This gap breaks Phase 1's GIF ("drag an element, see it published") and Phase 5's exit criterion ("a person without the author's help installs Portaless and publishes a site").

1. In the self-host Node runtime (which already serves `dist/` + `functions/`), serve `/pages/[slug]` dynamically from the `pages` table when the slug exists in the DB — static JSON files keep working as fallback, so nothing breaks for GitHub Pages.
2. A "Publish" button in the editor that runs the dump+build through the runtime (or, minimally, marks the page served-directly so the UI cycle closes without a rebuild).
3. Page manager minimum in the admin UI: list existing pages (`HttpPageStore.list()` already exists, it is just never called), open one by clicking, delete one (`onRequestDelete` does not exist yet in `functions/admin/pages/[slug].js`).

**Status: shipped (self-host + Cloudflare Pages).** `/pages/[slug]` is now served dynamically from the `pages` table by a new public function (`functions/pages/[slug].js`): when the slug exists in the DB it renders the stored layout through the atomic-elements engine inside the standard page shell, and when the slug is absent (or the stored layout is corrupt) it calls `context.next()` so the static build in `dist/` keeps working — on GitHub Pages nothing changes, and there the edited page still requires `npm run publish` (the honest static-hosting limit, documented in the CHANGELOG). The editor's save button is now **Publicar** and, after a successful save, shows a "Ver página pública ↗" link straight to `/pages/<slug>` — closing the edit→publish cycle in the UI. A minimum page manager landed at `/admin/pages`: it lists every page with its title (from the extended `GET /admin/api/pages`), opens one in the editor on click, and deletes it via a new `DELETE /admin/pages/:slug` (session + `canWrite`, `404 {removed:false}` when nothing was there). The list endpoint lives under `/admin/api/` rather than `/admin/pages/` because a Cloudflare Pages Function shadows the static HTML at the same path. Verified by unit tests (route hit/miss/corrupt/traversal, delete 401/403/200/404, title listing), a self-host e2e test proving the DB wins over the static build and that deleting a row re-exposes the static page, and a Playwright spec walking the full cycle (publish → public URL → edit → public URL updates without rebuild → manager delete → public URL 404s).

**Exit criterion**: a person edits a page in `/admin/editor`, clicks save/publish, opens the public URL in a new tab, and sees the change — on self-host, with no terminal open.

### Phase 4 — A second real implementer of APW

**Status**: items 1–2 shipped in this repository (starter kit + `@portaless/apw-resolver` prepared for independent publication; the `npm publish` itself is an operator step requiring npm credentials). Items 3–4 require people outside this repository — kindred projects to approach and authors to receive proposals from; they cannot be advanced by code in this repo alone. Item 5 (did:web Path A) stays blocked on its subject-mapping erratum, which is not yet published.

**Goal**: APW stops being a protocol with a single implementation — the minimal condition to aspire to the reference level.

**Why the hardest and most important phase**: no amount of additional code in this repo solves this. It is solved by getting someone external, without close coordination with the author, to read only `APW-SPEC-v1.3.md` and `APW-VECTORS-v1.3.md` and build an independent implementation that verifies against the same vectors.

1. **[shipped]** A minimal starter kit for external implementers: [`docs/protocol-apw/STARTER-KIT.md`](./docs/protocol-apw/STARTER-KIT.md) — language-agnostic, pseudocode for every step (fingerprint, TXT, did.json, chain, JWS/JCS, scoring), acceptance criteria = vectors V-01/03/05/06/08/09, no Portaless code required.
2. **[shipped in-repo]** Publish the simplest possible use case (resolving `did:apw` and verifying a manifest) as a standalone library: `packages/apw-resolver`'s `.` export is now a zero-workspace-dependency verifier surface (only `canonicalize` + Web APIs), with its own build (`dist/standalone.js` + types), README documenting "does not require the rest of Portaless", and package metadata ready for independent publication. The publish itself (`npm publish`) is an operator step with npm credentials.
3. **[requires external people]** Actively seek 1–2 kindred projects or communities (independent media, thematic wiki networks — the exact use cases spec v1.0 already identified in its section 11) and propose they implement APW on their own stack, with direct support from the author. **Current limit**: this requires approaching and coordinating with people outside this repository; the starter kit and package above are the artifacts that make the proposal possible, not a substitute for making it.
4. **[requires external channel]** Document publicly the change-proposal process for the spec (already present in v1.3's License and governance section: errata → version → proposals) with a real, visible channel to receive them. **Current limit**: this repository's public GitHub issues are the visible channel that exists today; growing it into an active external input flow requires people participating from outside.
5. **Optional did:web-compatible publication (Path A, recommended)**: keep the `_apw.<domain>` DNS TXT record as the root of the trust chain — a stronger root than an HTTPS file because it requires DNS control, not just hosting — and additionally publish an optional `/.well-known/did.json` in **did:web** format whose `service` entry points at the already DNS-anchored log. Any existing did:web resolver reads the identity unchanged; a client that does not know APW ignores the service it does not understand; a client that does know APW follows the service and fetches the trust chain. **One erratum is required before this ships**: define the subject mapping — attestations and history reference `did:apw:<domain>` subjects, so the erratum must state that `did:web:<domain>` publishing the same key and declaring the `did:apw:<domain>` log via `service` is recognized as the same subject for verification. Without that rule, "compatible" is half-true.

**Exit criterion**: at least one domain that is not the author's publishes a valid `_apw.*` record, verifiable with `resolveApwManifest()`, built with implementation code the author did not write.

### Phase 5 — Index as a real aggregator, public Beta

**Status**: items 1, 2, 4 and 5 shipped in this repository (A.4.4-style calibration, inter-node peer sync, the non-technical 10-minute install guide, and the public Beta announcement with its honesty note — see the annotated items below). Item 3 (onboarding tested with 3–5 people without a technical profile) requires real people and recorded sessions; it cannot be advanced by code alone. The exit criterion — an outside person installing Portaless unaided, publishing an APW site that appears in an Index node that is not the author's — stays open until that happens.

**Goal**: Index starts behaving like a real network, not a single-operator script, and the project opens its first official Beta with onboarding proven by real non-technical people.

**Why last**: Index aggregating witnesses of a single operator is not a network, it is a database. It needs Phase 4 to have produced more than one real instance first — aggregating witnesses of a single author would simulate a network, not build one.

1. **[shipped]** Witness weight calibration (A.4.4-style): each witness gains/loses a calibration factor by agreeing/disagreeing with the weighted consensus at every sync (`witnessCalibrationFactor`: Laplace-smoothed agreement rate mapped to ~0.1–~1.9, effective weight = base × factor, clamped; witnesses without an observation at the consensus seq are never counted; both components published per observation so any reader can recompute). Erratum E-12 documents it.
2. **[shipped]** Minimal inter-node synchronization: peer Index nodes are configured per node (`witness_index_peers`); `POST /admin/api/index/sync-peers` pulls each peer's `apw-index.json` and uses its entries as **discovery** of (subject, witnesses), re-running the full fail-closed verification pipeline locally — a peer never injects unverified observations (capped at 50 subjects / 20 witnesses per peer per sync). Erratum E-12 documents it.
3. **[requires real people]** The one-click onboarding wizard (already designed in this ROADMAP) tested with at least 3–5 people without a technical profile, with adjustments based on those sessions, not on design assumptions. **Current limit**: this requires recruiting and recording real sessions; the code path exists end to end (setup → wizard → identity → publish) and the non-technical guide (item 4) is the artifact those sessions should start from.
4. **[shipped]** Documentation for a non-technical audience: [`docs/first-steps.md`](./docs/first-steps.md) — “install your site in 10 minutes” in Spanish (voseo), Docker + browser only, reusing the Phase 1 screenshots/GIF, with an explicit honest-limits section (terminal step on GitHub Pages publish, single maintainer, exit criterion not met).
5. **[shipped]** Public Beta announcement: [`/beta`](./src/pages/beta.astro) (static, palette via the official tokens, live on the demo) plus the README “Public Beta” section — demo as first impression, what's ready vs not, links to the guide/ROADMAP/SECURITY; the README's stale “Index: design/spec stage only” claims were corrected to the real shipped status.

**Exit criterion (and close of this roadmap)**: a person outside the project, without the author's direct help, installs Portaless, publishes a site with APW identity, and that site appears correctly in at least one Index node that is not the author's.

### Phase 6 — Publication maturity (pre-Beta gap closers)

**Status**: items 1–4 shipped in this repository; the open element catalog that item 7 needs as an enabler is also shipped (v0.0.9.11), though the paywall plugin itself is not core. Items 5–7 are ordered work; none of them depends on a third party (item 5 needs real people, not code).

**Goal**: a Portaless site is a *publication*, not only a page builder: it is discoverable by feed readers, its pages (including the ones edited in the database) are visible to LLM agents, and the operator has first-party traffic numbers without sending visitors to somebody else's dashboard.

**Why after Phase 1**: features only matter once someone can install, edit and publish without help. This phase is about what happens *after* the first publish, and it is ordered strictly by cost/benefit: the first three are small and self-contained, the rest are not.

1. **[shipped]** RSS feed at `/rss.xml` for published posts (no drafts), announced from every page via `<link rel="alternate" type="application/rss+xml">` and from the `Optional` block of `llms.txt`. **Current limit**: `content:encoded` (full post body as HTML) is not emitted — the repo has no markdown→HTML renderer at build time and adding one only for the feed is not worth the dependency; readers get title, permalink, publication date and description.
2. **[shipped]** `llms.txt` lists the pages edited in D1/SQLite, not only `src/content/pages/*.json`. On a host with functions (self-host, Cloudflare Pages) a runtime function merges the database pages into the build-time file as their own section, deduplicated by slug, and honours the content-policy block signal. **Current limit**: on pure static hosting (GitHub Pages) the database is unreachable, so only the JSON pages appear — same honest limit as `/pages/[slug]` and documented in both files. The sitemap still lists only the JSON pages.
3. **[shipped]** First-party analytics, provider-agnostic: Plausible, Cloudflare Web Analytics, umami or any custom script URL, configured from `/admin/analytics`. The config lives in the new `site_settings` table (D1 → SQLite → InMemory), the public site reads it from `/api/analytics` at request time, and the build emits a no-op file for static hosting. **Current limit**: the admin can store a script URL and a site id, never raw JavaScript — that is a deliberate barrier, not a missing feature.
4. **[shipped; publication hardening C06, revision history C23–C26]** MCP content schemas remain derived from `elementRegistry`; `list_page_components` exposes the catalog; `insert_block`/`update_block`/`delete_block` edit atomic nodes. A single `isPubliclyPublishable()` policy excludes draft, private, legacy `-draft` and unknown states from static routes/translations, Explore, sitemap, `llms.txt`, RSS and Git publish. The runtime `/pages/:slug` returns 404 `no-store` for DB draft/private rows instead of falling through to stale static HTML. MCP creates drafts and cannot mutate published/private pages; admin publication writes are revisioned and protected by ETags. Approved revisions export under `/revisions/<publicationId>/index.json` and can be imported with IDs/hashes preserved. **Limits**: legacy `-draft` rows are not migrated; revision manifests are unsigned; exact-page reputation inputs do not exist, so each revision reports `no_data`; collaborative in-canvas agent UX remains out of scope.
**C06 update — supersedes the publication claims and limits in sub-item 4 above.** `PageLayout.status` includes `private`; the central `isPubliclyPublishable()` policy filters public static/runtime routes, locale versions, Explore, sitemap, RSS, `llms.txt` and the Git publish CLI. The dynamic Node route returns 404 `no-store` for draft/private DB rows without falling through to stale HTML. MCP `create_page` is draft-only; MCP/plugin reads omit non-public pages; MCP/plugin writes cannot publish or mutate published/private pages. C07 preview and C08 clean allowlisted export/stale-artifact withdrawal are shipped. **C23–C26** add revision identities, approved public history, ETag concurrency, transfer import and a page-level revision UI; see the initial implementation limits below. Legacy `-draft` rows are not migrated.

**C23–C26 — public revision history (initial implementation).** `pages` keeps the current mutable head; `page_revisions` stores append-only snapshots with publication UUID, revision UUID/number, content hash, parent, timestamps and approval metadata. SQLite triggers reject SQL updates/deletes. Admin page writes use ETags/If-Match; stale writes fail with 409 and creates use If-None-Match. `GET /revisions/<publicationId>/index.json` and the clean Git publisher expose approved revisions only; drafts, private rows, private parents and admin usernames are omitted. `/admin/api/revisions/import` verifies content hashes and public parent links and preserves exported IDs/timestamps on the new publisher. The page UI shows dates, changed JSON paths and the exact content hash. **Evidence limit:** APW observations today identify a site DID, not a page revision. Therefore page reputation remains `no_data`; this work does not change APW's normative formats or claim unsigned JSON/Git history proves authorship or tamper-proof storage. A real revision-bound observation source is still required before any score can be attached.

5. **[requires real people]** The exit criterion of Phase 5. It is repeated here because it is the real gate between "Beta announced" and "official Beta", and no item in this phase replaces it.
6. **[deferred]** Newsletters and a subscriber store: double opt-in, unsubscribe, deliverability. The MCP→provider send is the cheap part; the store and the compliance are not, and there is no audience to send to yet. AT Protocol is a **discovery** channel for it (map followers → invite them), never a delivery channel: AT Protocol does not deliver email, and the follower graph lives in third-party AppViews.
7. **[plugin, not core]** Paywall / premium content. Two hard rules from this repository's own architecture: (a) premium content must be **rendered at the edge at request time**, never committed as static HTML — static premium bytes are public in the repo and in every deploy, so no paywall can protect them; (b) the billing provider is chosen by the operator (Stripe, Webpay, Mercado Pago, …) through a **plugin** with capability tokens. Portaless core ships no billing provider and no subscription economy: the economy that is already in core is pay-per-crawl, and a second one would be a parallel system, not a feature. **Shipped enabler (v0.0.9.11)**: the *open element catalog* that lets a paywall plugin exist at all — `ElementPack` (declarative, data-only: schema + `html-template`, never a render function), `ElementPackStore` (D1 → SQLite → in-memory, seeded with a `demo.Badge`), `resolveCatalog(core, packs)`, `renderPage` with `static`/`request` modes (a `requiresEdgeEval` node emits a structural placeholder in `static` that never touches its `props`, so premium bytes cannot reach `dist/`), `validatePageLayout(layout, catalog?)`, call-time catalog resolution in the MCP tools, and the editor palette/panel/preview over the resolved catalog. Design in [`docs/architecture/element-catalog-plugins.md`](./docs/architecture/element-catalog-plugins.md); 25 tests in `tests/unit/element-catalog-plugins.test.ts`. **Still not built (second pass / the plugin itself)**: `requiresEdgeEval` end-to-end through the sandbox, `secretProps` with the build-time guard, the pack-installation UI, and the paywall plugin (gating + billing) — all of which live in the operator's plugin, not in core.

## After the official Beta — phases 7 to 11: from a product to a standard

> **None of this gates the Beta.** Phases 0–6 ask *can one person run this?*; phases 7–11 ask a different question: *what would have to be true for Protocol APW to be a standard the rest of the web builds on, rather than one implementation with a spec attached?* Phase 11 says so in its own text — it **blocks nothing in phases 7–10** — and the exit criteria of 8, 9 and 10 depend on operators who do not exist yet. Splitting these two questions here is deliberate: conflating them is what makes a roadmap promise a network it cannot deliver alone.

### How these five connect

**Status (2026-10-15): every phase here has something shipped, and none of them is closed.** Phase 7 shipped (conformance: **110/110 cases over 11 vectors**, verified 2026-10-15, plus the clean-room tarball check); Phase 8 shipped its mechanism, with the corpus still external; Phase 9 shipped the ranking (V-16) and the federation mechanism (E-14) with **no public endpoint and no real peers**; Phase 10 shipped the honesty cleanup and the activity feed, with the modules still open; Phase 11 shipped item 1 (MIT SDK 0.1.0) and the mechanism of item 2 (installer **behind a flag, off by default**), and item 3 remains deliberately unshipped. The pattern across all five is the same and is worth naming once: **the code is not what is missing — the operators are.** The repository boundary that governs these phases is [`docs/architecture/ecosystem-boundaries.md`](./docs/architecture/ecosystem-boundaries.md): the protocols, the kernel and the content modules stay here under AGPL; Cloudum, LinsWork, Tenantless and Ecomless are separate repositories.

**Why these five, and why in this order.** They are ordered by dependency, not by appeal. The chain is real and circular at one point, so it has to be broken at the cheapest link:

```
installable package + executable conformance suite
        ↓
a second implementer can build against the spec without reading this code
        ↓
that implementer emits reader_conduct attestations  →  a corpus exists
        ↓
R-05 becomes computable  →  evaluator weights become real
        ↓
the Sybil defenses in spec §6.7 have something to defend against
        ↓
a Veracier's reputation means something, and its history is worth publishing
```

Phase 7 breaks that circle because it is the cheapest link in it. Phases 8 and 9 are the substance of the trust web. Phase 10 is adoption. Phase 11 is the commercial seam and **blocks nothing in phases 7–10** — if it slips, APW is still a standard; if phases 7–9 slip, it is not.

**What none of these phases needs**: another architecture document. The boundaries, the licensing argument and the enabling-pieces inventory are already written down. What is missing is code and third parties.

#### Phase 7 — Executable conformance

**Status: shipped (2026-10-15).** The spec's claim is now checked by CI instead of asserted.

**Goal**: §5.6 of the spec claims another implementation can verify compatibility "without depending on Portaless's code". Today that claim is unfalsifiable: `APW-VECTORS-v1.3.md` is 22 KB of markdown and **no workflow runs it**.

1. **[shipped]** Extract the published vectors into machine-readable fixtures, keeping the markdown as the human-readable form. **Deviation from the original wording**: the fixtures live in `docs/protocol-apw/vectors/v1.3/` (CC BY 4.0), **not inside the npm package**. Shipping them in the code distribution would undercut the very claim they exist to support — that they are independent of the code. The published runner takes a path to them instead.
2. **[shipped]** A CI job that runs the reference implementation against every fixture: `tests/unit/apw-conformance-vectors.test.ts`, 7 tests. It shipped with **78/78 cases passing** across the eight vectors that existed then (V-01, V-02, V-03, V-04, V-05, V-08, V-09, V-12); Phase 8 added V-14/V-15 and Phase 9a added V-16, so the same suite runs **110/110 cases over 11 vectors** today (measured 2026-10-15). The runner imports **only** from the package's public entry point, so a missing export fails CI. **What this exposed and fixed**: `buildApwManifest` and the whole scoring surface (`issuerWeight`, `judgeFromDeviation`, `toValue`, `bayesianScore`, `calibrateJudges`, `scoreSubject`, `scoreOf`, …) were not exported at all, so the spec's *minimal interoperability vectors* V-08 and V-09 were not implementable from the published package.
3. **[shipped]** A CI job that packs the real tarball, installs it into an empty project and imports it: `.github/workflows/apw-package-conformance.yml`, exercising both `.` (39 exports) and `./full` (13 exports), then running the published `bin` against the vectors.
4. **[shipped]** A published runner: `bin: apw-conformance`, so a third party runs `npx @portaless/apw-resolver <path-to-vectors>` against its own implementation.

**Exit criterion**: a party with no access to this repository can run the suite and obtain a pass/fail against its own implementation, for the published spec version. **Met**, verified in a clean room (rsync without `node_modules` → `npm ci` → build → pack → install → run the bin).

**Honest limits, recorded rather than hidden.**
- **V-06 is not executable yet**, so the minimal interoperability set is *not fully* satisfied: it is the only required vector absent from the fixture set, and the test asserts exactly that (`expect(missing).toEqual(["V-06"])`) so the gap cannot regress into silence. Its 12-step validation order lives in `packages/trust-layer/src/site-trust/attestation.ts` (`verifyAttestation`), not in `apw-resolver`.
- **V-07, V-10, V-11 and V-13 are not yet machine-readable**, and say so in `vectors/v1.3/manifest.json` under `notYetMachineReadable`, each with its reason. V-07 and V-13 are byte-level mutation matrices with a deterministic `jti` preimage; V-10 needs per-subject attestation fixtures; V-11 needs a database and a non-pure decision function.
- **A spec-level precision was found and documented**: the vector document prints `w(6.7) = 0.9025` as "exact", but in IEEE-754 doubles `((6.7-1)/6)^2` is `0.9025000000000001`, one ULP above it. Every numeric comparison in the fixtures and the runner uses a tolerance, and `v-08-scoring-primitives.json` states the rule under `comparisonRule` — otherwise a correct implementation would fail a strict equality check against a correct one.

#### Phase 8 — Bind identity to reputation

**Status: shipped (2026-10-15), with one recorded limit.** The trust graph now has verifiable authors, but the *corpus* it needs is still a fact about the world, not code.

**Goal**: make the trust graph mean something by giving every evaluation a verifiable author.

The data model is already in place and was verified to exist: `site_trust_community_votes` (`schema.sql:319`) stores a `score` constrained to 1–5, a free-text `comment`, `voted_at`, and one row per category and voter. The scoring maths is real too — `bayesianScore` with `NEUTRAL = 4.0` and `PRIOR_WEIGHT = 3`, on top of witness calibration and weighted consensus. What is missing is the one thing that makes it a graph rather than a form.

1. Replace `voter_id` (`anon-${crypto.randomUUID()}` in `localStorage`, `src/pages/trust/index.astro:63`) with a vote signed by the voter's `did:apw`, so an emitter is attributable and its `jti` is unique per issuer.
2. Implement spec §6.7 with real data: evaluator weighting (0.25 for a new domain, gained only by agreeing with existing consensus), distinct emitters only, `min_weight` per predicate, minimum history age (W-07), and self-attestations never counting toward the emitter's own R-05.
3. A public page per Veracier carrying its emission history.
4. **Errata, not a version change** (decided 2026-10-15): APW v1.3 stands. Scores, counts and weight sums stay always public (already normative, §6.6); **the comment text stays governed** (§A.6), so it is visible to the emitter and to readers who meet the site's policy, not to everyone. The errata states this explicitly and names the Veracier page as a described surface. Making review text universally public would change what being reviewed means for every future subject and would alter the Sybil calculus — that is a v1.4 decision, deliberately not taken here.

**Exit criterion**: `R-05` is computable from a corpus of at least two independent emitters, and no vote can be emitted without a signature. **Met for the second half; the first half is not a code deliverable.** A vote cannot be emitted without a signature: `POST /trust/:siteId/community-attestation` verifies a JWS against the emitter's resolved `did:apw` identity (§5.1) and the old `/vote` route now answers `410`. But "at least two independent emitters" is a fact about operators, and today there is exactly one (`SECURITY.md`: the Beta is invite-only for want of a second maintainer). A test fixture with three keys proves the mechanism, not that three operators exist.

**What shipped, and what did not.**
1. **[shipped]** `voter_id` is gone. A community evaluation is a JWS signed by a `did:apw` (ERRATA E-13), with `jti` unique per issuer (`iss, jti` primary key). Storage is a new append-only table `community_attestations` — re-emitting adds a row, and the scoring takes the latest per `(iss, subject, category)`, so a history exists and cannot be quietly overwritten. `site_trust_community_votes` is left untouched as the visitor-opinion table: it stays visible and **no longer scores**, because it has no verifiable identity.
2. **[shipped]** §6.7 has real definitions (E-13): a **distinct emitter** is a distinct `did:apw`; the community predicate's **`min_weight` is 2.0 applied per (subject, category)**; **minimum emitter history age is a 7-day threshold**, fail-closed when the history cannot be resolved. Calibration, distinct-emitter counting and self-exclusion already existed and are now exercised against signed rows. Vectors **V-14** and **V-15** make the predicate executable from the published package (conformance reached 97/97 over 10 vectors at this point).
3. **[shipped, with a scope limit]** `/trust/veracier/<domain>` publishes counts, averages and the last emission per category for that emitter — a JSON surface with an HTML shell. **Limit**: it shows what *this site* recorded from that emitter, not the emitter's global history, which lives in its own APW chain (§5.4) and is not fetched here. This is a direct consequence of choosing the evaluated site's table over the emitter's chain (see the decision recorded in E-13).
4. **[shipped]** The errata is written (E-13), APW v1.3 stands, and comment text stays governed (A.6): the Veracier page publishes aggregates, never the text. The rule that makes this work is stated in the spec's Status line, beside the §5.3 `src` enum, and in §6.7.

**Consequences worth stating plainly, because they are the real shape of this phase.**
- **`min_weight` 2.0 is strict.** §6.7 asks for a minimum weight sum, and A.4.2 gives a cold-start emitter 0.25, so a category needs **eight** cold-start domains — or fewer well-calibrated ones — to clear it. Two emitters that agree calibrate each other to J = 7 and reach exactly 2.0; two that disagree do not clear it at all. This was found while writing the tests, not in review. It is the intended trade: fewer, harder votes over many cheap ones, and the honest cost is that a young network scores almost nothing.
- **The real Sybil cost is the 7-day history floor plus the price of domains, not the weight.** `calibrateJudges` awards high weight to a set of emitters that agree with each other even when all are new, so a cabal is a "consensus". §6.7 already says no defense is perfect and that the cost of building old, calibrated domains is the defense; this phase does not change that, it just makes the floor explicit.

#### Phase 9 — Federated query

**Goal**: the network becomes usable without becoming a directory anyone controls.

**Status (2026-10-15): 9a (ranking) and 9b (federation mechanism) shipped; the public endpoint and the exit criterion are open.** The ordering and the document are code-complete and fixture-checked; no node publishes `apw-pages.jws` yet because that needs operators, not code — so `Explore` still reads only its own content and says so in the UI.

1. **[mechanism shipped; endpoint deliberately not opened]** `Explore` reads other nodes over APW. The mechanism lives in `packages/apw-resolver/src/federation/`: the signed pages document `/.well-known/apw-pages.jws` (`typ apw-pages+jws`, ERRATA **E-14**), the hardened fetch (`redirect: "manual"` + per-hop validation, streaming byte cap, private-range blocking), sanitized remote render (scheme check, item cap, no `set:html`) and a peer aggregator with **mandatory cache + single-flight + concurrency cap + stale-while-error**. It is verified by `tests/unit/apw-federation.test.ts` against fixtures, **without opening a public endpoint**. What is not shipped: the public `/explore.json.js` and a real peer list — an unauthenticated endpoint that fans out to N peers only has value once peers exist, and its whole contract is testable without it. `Explore` keeps the honest degraded state from 9a.
2. **[shipped]** Normative definitions for every exposed filter (`Top`, `Recents`, `Confidence`, `Old`, `New`, `History`) in spec §7.1, executable as vector **V-16**: `Top` is the prior-weighted Bayesian score over a declared window, `Confidence` is the witness consensus over the declared `seq`, the order is a total order with a full tie-break tuple, and an empty subject is never filled with the neutral prior. `EmissionPeriod` still covers `month | week | day`; spec §7.1 states that a year range aggregates the weekly buckets (no `year` period).
3. **[shipped]** A third party can recompute the ordering from the same signed attestations — the point of §7.1 + V-16 (the definition is in the spec, not only in the AGPL code, so a closed product can implement it).

**Exit criterion**: two independently operated nodes, neither operated by the author, can each produce the same ordering for the same signed attestations. **Open**: two independently operated nodes is a fact about operators. The ordering (V-16) and the document (E-14) are recomputable and testable from fixtures, but real operators do not exist yet.

**Goal**: adoption. Ordered by adoption value, not by dependency; none of it is a precondition for a standard.

**Status (2026-10-15): the honesty cleanup shipped; the modules did not.**

2. **Recents** — *partially shipped*. #### Phase 11 — The commercial seam

**Goal**: everything a closed module needs to exist, without touching the core's license.

1. **[shipped]** `packages/plugin-sdk` — the MIT-licensed typed surface, published as **0.1.0** (2026-10-15). It re-declares its own types, capability catalog and `validateManifest`, importing nothing from the core; equivalence is pinned by an AGPL-side test (`tests/unit/plugin-sdk-parity.test.ts`, deep-equal of the catalog and `validateManifest` parity). Its tarball is checked by `.github/workflows/plugin-sdk-conformance.yml` (pack → install into an empty project → `tsc --noEmit` over a consumer that uses every export + runtime import). **Honest scope**: the capability surface is provisional until item 3 closes (storage quotas, backend error codes); the README says so. The licensing path is recorded in `CONTRIBUTING.md` (copyright ownership note + DCO + the one-way AGPL→MIT rule).
2. **[shipped, behind a flag]** An HTTP installer for plugins. Shipped (2026-10-15, runtime wired in §14.1): `POST /admin/api/plugins/install` (and `uninstall`) behind `PORTALESS_PLUGIN_INSTALLER`, **off by default** (404 without it, no announcement). It requires step-up, verifies a **publisher-signed manifest** against the resolved `did:apw` identity (ERRATA **E-15**, `typ apw-plugin-manifest+jws`, the signature covers `requestedCapabilities`), stores the code **chunked** in BLOB (≤512 KB/fila, hard cap declared, never base64 — `plugin_artifacts`/`plugin_artifact_chunks`) **together with its verified manifest** (`manifest_json`, self-migrated on existing tables), records the active `version` in `plugin_registry`, and **grants nothing**: install registers, the grant stays `granted ∩ manifest`. Immutability is `(publisher, name, version) → (code hash, manifest)` (a re-install with different bytes or a different manifest is `409`), and updates reconcile grants: removed capabilities are **pruned**, expanded ones **return to review** (`packages/plugin-sandbox/src/install/reconcile.ts`). `POST /admin/api/plugins/run` resolves `{ manifest, code }` from `PluginArtifactStore` first (re-checking `sha256Hex(code) === codeHash` before executing) and falls back to `PORTALESS_PLUGIN_DIR` on disk only when no stored artifact exists — closing the workerd `503 plugin_source_backend_unavailable` for installed plugins. The five plugin capabilities without a real execution path remain `501` by decision (item 3): `media:*`, `commerce:*` and `site:admin` need an external provider or a reviewed operation set, and are deliberately staged. (`network:fetch` is handled by each sandbox adapter, not by the host bridge.) `storage:read`/`storage:write` shipped in §14.2 and `agent:identify` right after (`content:read`/`content:write` were already real).
3. Backends for the capabilities that return `501 NOT_CONFIGURED`. **[in progress, one at a time]** `content:read`/`content:write` were real first; **`storage:read`/`storage:write` shipped in §14.2** (per-plugin namespace + quotas); **`agent:identify` shipped right after** (read-only over the Trust Layer usage ledger, the same data the public `/.well-known/portaless-usage-log.json` already publishes); **`email:send` shipped in the review round** (blind delegation: the plugin sends `{ template, to, data }`, the core approves the template, injects the site `from` and enforces a per-plugin daily quota). Remaining: `media:*`, `commerce:*`, `site:admin`. **Staged by decision** — the pattern stays the one already set (the core exposes the interface, the plugin supplies the implementation, as `SettlementProvider.charge()` returns `success: false`), and `site:admin` carries a **recorded decision to stay `501` until a concrete, reviewed use case exists**:
   - **`site:admin`** ("modify global site configuration", the highest-risk capability). The owner's need is already covered by the admin panel itself, under step-up; **no document defines which operations a plugin would be allowed to perform**, and granting the broadest capability to a sandboxed third party without that definition would be inventing attack surface. It stays `501` until a namespace-scoped operation set is specified and reviewed.
   - ~~**`email:send`**~~ **[superseded — shipped]** The recorded decision to keep it `501` was replaced by a concrete, reviewed design: **blind delegation** (the plugin sends only `{ template, to, data }`; the core approves the template, injects the site `from` and never lets the plugin set it), a **per-plugin daily quota** in the database (the rate limit, `plugin_email_quota`, migration v4) and a payload whitelist that rejects `from`/`cc`/`bcc`. The transport lives in `packages/email-transport` (webhook/SMTP); the separate `packages/auth/src/email-sender.ts` (step-up codes) is untouched. Shipped in commits `d0e6b2e`, `b9a8f00` and the review round (`2ac05cb` and after).
   `media:*` and `commerce:checkout` need an external provider (or a per-plugin media namespace) and are each their own step. The hard `501`/`403` assertions in `capability-bridge-security.test.ts` and `production-plugin-runner.test.ts` still hold for the ones without a backend.

**Exit criterion**: a closed module installs into a stock Portaless instance, runs under the sandbox, and is granted only capabilities a human approved. **Note**: Cloudum's relationship to the invite-only decision in [`SECURITY.md`](./SECURITY.md) is unresolved, and this phase does not decide it.

They carry the class from [`docs/VISION.md`](./docs/VISION.md): `NOW` (a defect), `ENABLE` (a barrier for the next participant), `GATED` (needs evidence that does not exist yet).
